## A Real Job Near Riley Lake

We worked on a home near Lake Riley where one valley-fed gutter section overflowed during every storm. The system wasn't clogged or broken. It was undersized for the volume that section received once several roof planes funneled water into it at once. When that valley overflowed, water moved downhill toward the home's walkout basement, and it eventually settled as moisture along the lower wall, which is when the homeowner first called us, months after the overflow itself had started.

That's the pattern we see most often in homes like this one around the Riley Lake area. The custom lake homes here sit on genuinely sloped lots, which means water doesn't just pool where it lands. Once it leaves an undersized gutter section, it keeps moving until it finds the lowest point, and on a lot with a walkout basement, that lowest point is often a patio door or a foundation seam.

## How Homeowners Here Get Help

Most calls start the way the Riley Lake job did: a homeowner notices dampness at a walkout level and assumes it's a foundation issue before anyone traces it back to the roof. We start at the gutter, not the basement wall. Tracing the water back to where it actually enters the system is what tells us whether this is a sealing problem below or a sizing problem above, and around here it's usually the roof.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

When a valley section overflows repeatedly and nothing changes, water keeps finding the same path downhill toward the same low point on the property, season after season. That repeated saturation is what eventually shows up as moisture inside the home rather than just dampness outside it, and by the time it's visible indoors, the fix usually involves more than the gutter alone.
