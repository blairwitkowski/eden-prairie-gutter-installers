## An Office Corridor Built in The 1980s

City West Parkway sits at the junction of Hwy 212 and Shady Oak Road, on the western edge of the Golden Triangle business park, and a lot of the office and medical-office buildings here date to the 1980s. A single-tenant medical office at 6446 City West Pkwy, for example, was built in 1984 with masonry construction, a fairly typical profile for this stretch.

Buildings from that era generally run flat or low-slope roofs, which means drainage happens through scuppers set into the parapet wall or internal drains running down through the roof deck, not a residential-style gutter along the roofline. When debris accumulates in one of those openings, water has nowhere to exit, which leads to ponding on the roof surface itself rather than overflow visible from the parking lot, and that standing water puts real weight and exposure on a membrane that wasn't built to hold it indefinitely. In a lot of cases, the first sign anyone notices is a ceiling stain inside the building, well after the ponding started.

## How Property Managers Here Get Help

We inspect scupper openings and internal drain points for debris, check for any sign of ponding on the roof deck, and look at whether the drainage system is keeping pace with the roof's actual runoff, especially important on a building this age where the original system may not have been sized for current storm patterns. Where a property does use a perimeter gutter, we service that the same way we would a residential system, just scaled to a commercial roofline.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

Ponding water on a flat roof doesn't resolve itself. Left in place through repeated storms, it accelerates membrane wear at that exact spot, which leads to a leak that shows up inside the building, and by the time it's visible on a ceiling tile, the roof membrane underneath has usually already been compromised for a while.
