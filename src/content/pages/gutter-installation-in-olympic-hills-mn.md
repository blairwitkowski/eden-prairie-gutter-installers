## Rolling Terrain, Not Just a Rolling Course

Olympic Hills is built around the private Olympic Hills Golf Club, a renovated course set in genuinely rolling terrain in the southwest part of Eden Prairie. Over time, what we usually see is that the same terrain shaping the fairways shapes the lots around them, and it's a factor we account for on every installation here.

Rolling terrain means a lot's natural drainage rarely runs in a straight line. When a downspout is placed at the standard corner without checking the actual grade, water discharges downhill and settles wherever the slope happens to carry it, which leads to pooling in a spot the homeowner never expected, sometimes against a garage slab, sometimes along a side yard, and that unexpected wet spot is usually the first sign anything is wrong.

## How Homeowners Here Get Help

We walk the lot as carefully as we walk the roofline. On a home set into a hillside, we look at where water naturally wants to go once it leaves the downspout and make sure the placement works with that grade rather than against it. Around here, water tends to move toward whichever low point sits closest, and that point is rarely the same for two houses on the same street.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

A downspout draining to the wrong spot doesn't announce itself right away. It shows up gradually, as a patch of yard that stays soft longer than it should after every storm, and eventually as settling near a walkway or slab edge. This is one of those problems that does not look urgent until the ground itself starts to shift.
