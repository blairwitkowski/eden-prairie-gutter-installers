## The Oldest Gutters in The City Are Probably Here

Northeast Eden Prairie, north of Hwy 5 near the Hopkins and Minnetonka border, holds some of the city's oldest housing stock, 1960s and 70s split-entry, rambler, and traditional two-story homes, many still under the mature tree canopy that's grown in over the decades since. The neighboring Edenvale and Eden Heights areas share that same mid-century to 1980s character.

Homes this age have often gone through at least one, sometimes two, prior gutter systems, and it's not uncommon to find a system that's been patched repeatedly rather than replaced. Patches hold for a while, but they're rarely a permanent fix once the fascia underneath has started to show wear. When a patch covers a weak spot without addressing what caused it, water finds a new way through eventually, which leads to the same repair call a year or two later, and that's usually when a homeowner decides it's time to stop patching and start over.

## How Homeowners Here Get Help

Before installing anything new, we check the fascia board the gutters attach to. On a home this age, wood that's been exposed to repeated water intrusion from a failing gutter can be soft or rotted, and hanging a new system onto compromised wood just sets up the next failure. If we find that, we'll tell you before doing anything, since fascia repair sometimes needs to happen first.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

This is one of those situations that does not look urgent from the curb. A gutter can look intact while the fascia behind it has already softened enough that the next heavy snow load pulls the whole section loose. Around here, that failure tends to happen suddenly, not gradually, once the wood underneath finally gives out.
