## The Newest Builds, The Biggest Rooflines

Settlers Ridge and the adjacent Reeder Ridge sit in the newest wave of custom home development along Eden Prairie's southwestern bluffs, with builds running from the late 1990s through the 2010s. In homes built during this period, architectural ambition shows up directly on the roof, more planes, more valleys, more places for water to concentrate before it ever reaches a gutter.

We see this most often in homes like these: a roofline with several intersecting planes sends more volume into a valley section during a storm than a simple gable roof ever would, and a gutter system sized off a generic estimate rather than the actual roof plan overflows at exactly that point. When that happens repeatedly, homeowners usually notice a pattern before they notice a cause, the same section, storm after storm.

## How Homeowners Here Get Help

Because a lot of these homes are relatively new, we're often working with builder-installed gutters sized for the project budget rather than the roof's actual drainage needs. We walk the roofline, identify every valley, and size each section for the water it will realistically carry during a heavy Minnesota storm, not just the average across the whole run.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

A single overflow event is easy to dismiss as an unusually hard storm. Around here, water tends to concentrate at the same undersized valley every time conditions repeat, and each pass adds a little more stress to the hangers holding that section in place until something eventually pulls loose.
