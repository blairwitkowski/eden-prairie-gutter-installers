## What Boulder Pointe's Terrain Does to a Gutter System

Boulder Pointe sits in the newer wave of southwestern bluff-line construction that has gone up in Eden Prairie over the past couple of decades, alongside neighboring Bearpath and Settlers Ridge. In homes built during this period, the roofline is often more architecturally complex than the lot itself, several planes, several valleys, and a builder-installed gutter system sized to a standard spec rather than the roof actually in front of the crew.

Around here, water tends to move downhill and keep moving once it leaves a gutter section, because these are sloped lots that follow the natural grade of the bluffs rather than a flat, graded subdivision. When a downspout discharges without accounting for that slope, water does not simply soak into the yard, it travels, and it often ends up right back at the lower part of the house, which is where homeowners usually notice it first, as dampness along a walkout basement wall or a patio door threshold.

## How Homeowners Here Get Help

Most calls come after a homeowner has already tried to solve the problem at ground level, regrading a bed, adding a splash block, before realizing the water is coming from the roof in the first place. We start at the roofline, not the yard. We walk every valley, note where the current gutter and downspouts sit relative to the lot's actual slope, and only then talk about what needs to change.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

When a valley section is undersized, water overflows there during heavier storms, that overflow runs downhill along the bluff's natural grade, and it concentrates at the lowest point on the property, which on a walkout lot is usually right against the foundation. Left alone through another two or three Minnesota winters, that repeated saturation works into fascia from above and into foundation seams from below at the same time.

## Homes, Roofs, And Drainage Realities in Boulder Pointe

Boulder Pointe homes run roughly $650K to $1.7M, part of the late 1990s through 2010s executive-build era that defines this corridor. That build era favored dramatic rooflines with multiple gables and valleys, which is exactly the roof type that needs individual valley sizing rather than a single gutter width run around the whole house.

This is one of those situations that does not look urgent until a wet season makes it obvious. A gutter that handled three dry summers without complaint can still be undersized for the one storm that hits hardest, and on a bluff lot, that single storm is often what finally shows the problem.
