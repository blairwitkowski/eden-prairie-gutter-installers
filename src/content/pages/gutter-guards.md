## What's included

We carry and install four gutter guard systems: **Leaf Sentry**, **Bulldog Gutter Guard**, **Leaf Blaster**, and **XTREME Gutter Guards**. Which one makes sense for your home depends on your tree cover, your roof pitch, and how much you want to spend, not a single default we push on everyone.

- Guard selection matched to your tree cover, from light seasonal drop to heavy year-round canopy
- Correct pitch and fastening for your specific roofline, not a standard angle off a chart
- Proper mounting that doesn't compromise the shingle edge above it
- Extra sizing at valley sections where volume is higher than a straight run
- An honest read on which system fits, including when a cheaper option is genuinely fine

## What usually goes wrong

Most gutter guard complaints trace back to a few real patterns. Water sheeting over the top edge during a hard storm usually means a valley section is receiving more volume than the guard in that spot was sized for. We see this most on the steep, multi-valley rooflines common in [Bearpath](/gutter-installation-in-bearpath-mn/) and [Settlers Ridge](/gutter-installation-in-settlers-ridge-mn/), where a guard cut to a standard width just can't keep up with what one valley is funneling into it. A different cause is a fine mesh surface that's built up a film of pollen, tree sap, and shingle grit over a few seasons and started shedding water across the top instead of letting it through, more common under the heavier tree canopy near [Bryant Lake](/gutter-installation-in-bryant-lake-mn/) and the older, mature-canopy lots toward [Northeast Eden Prairie](/gutter-installation-in-northeast-eden-prairie-mn/). None of that means the guard has failed. It means it needs resizing at that section or a cleaning pass.

A separate issue is guards that sag or cave into the trough. That's usually cheap fasteners or a frame that wasn't built for real snow load, not the mesh itself. A guard that's properly fastened for Minnesota winters shouldn't need that kind of attention for years.

There's also a marketing claim worth addressing directly: "maintenance-free." No guard is fully maintenance-free. Fine mesh systems cut debris down dramatically, but they still need an occasional check and clean as organic film builds up. We'd rather tell you that upfront than have you find out on your own after buying into a claim that oversold it.

## Winter and ice

Ice on a gutter guard behaves differently than ice on an open gutter. It builds along the cold metal edge and can hang heavy over a walkway below, and if the trough underneath is also frozen and blocked, that combined weight is what eventually pulls a gutter section away from the fascia over a hard winter. We fasten guards to handle real snow and ice load, not just a dry-season install.
