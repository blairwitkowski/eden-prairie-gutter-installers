## The Newest, Largest Rooflines in The City

Southwest Eden Prairie is where a lot of the city's newest construction has gone up, custom executive builds from the late 1990s through the 2010s that include Bearpath, Reeder Ridge, and Settlers Ridge. This corridor sits along the Minnesota River bluffs, and US-212 runs through it as the main commuter route toward Chanhassen and Carver County.

Bluff terrain plus large, architecturally complex homes is a specific combination for gutter work. These rooflines tend to have more valleys and intersecting planes than an older, simpler home design, and the sloped lots mean water that isn't captured and redirected correctly at the roofline keeps moving once it hits the ground. When a valley on a roof like this is undersized, water overflows there during a heavy storm, runs downhill along the bluff's natural grade, and settles at whatever low point the lot provides, and that low point is often right against the foundation on a walkout lot.

## How Homeowners Here Get Help

We treat every home in this part of the city as a full valley-sizing exercise rather than a standard estimate. That means walking the roofline to count planes and valleys, then sizing each gutter section for the volume it will actually carry, not an average across the whole run. On the sloped lots common here, downspout placement gets the same individual attention.

## Why Waiting Makes it Worse

This is one of those problems that does not look urgent until the one storm that finally exposes it. A gutter system can handle three unremarkable summers without complaint and still be undersized for the storm that hits hardest, and on a bluff lot, that single event is often what finally makes the problem visible, usually as water somewhere it's never been before.
