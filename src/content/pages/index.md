## Built For How Water Actually Moves on Eden Prairie Roofs

A lot of gutter problems here aren't about the gutter itself. They're about where the roof sends water in the first place. Eden Prairie has more steep, multi-level rooflines than a flat suburb has any right to, especially in the newer bluff-line neighborhoods to the southwest. Multiple roof planes meeting at a valley concentrate a storm's worth of water into one narrow section. If that section isn't sized to handle the surge, it overflows even when the rest of the system is clean and working fine.

On sloped lots with walkout basements, that overflow doesn't just sit in the yard. It follows the grade downhill toward patio doors and foundation seams, and it comes back after every storm until the drainage is actually fixed. We size and pitch for that, not just for whatever came standard when the house was built.

Winters here add their own math. Freeze-thaw cycles are hard on hangers and seams, and they're the main reason a gutter that looked fine in October is pulling away from the fascia by March. Custom-formed gutters, fabricated on-site instead of joined from stock lengths, hold up better against that cycle because there are fewer seams for ice to work loose.
